# BoomVoyage

Fireworks from around the world, every 2 minutes.

A single static web page. It draws a night waterfront on a canvas and plays a
fireworks show about every two minutes, with sound made live in the browser.
There are four places: Sydney Harbour (the default), Dubai, Paris and London.
More can be added one file at a time.

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No frameworks, no build step, no libraries,
and no image, audio, video or font files. Everything you see and hear is
generated by code.

## What it does

- Four full-screen night scenes: Sydney Harbour, Dubai, Paris and London, each
  with sky, stars, skyline and water with moving reflections.
- Each place has its own sky colours, colour themes, act names, kind of show
  and one signature effect on its landmark.
- A fireworks show every 2 minutes, with a "Next show in m:ss" countdown.
- Every show is different: new colours, new middle acts, new timing.
- A signature act and a finale in every show: the bridge waterfall in Sydney,
  sparks from the Burj Khalifa's spire in Dubai, glitter on the Eiffel Tower
  in Paris, a ring of bursts around the London Eye in London.
- Sound for every launch, burst and crackle, plus a soft night ambience.
- Life between shows: while the countdown runs, small things happen in every
  place (a ferry crosses, a plane blinks past, the tower sparkles, a bus
  crosses the bridge). They fade away before the fireworks start.
- Mute button and a volume slider (both remembered), fullscreen button, "Launch now" button.
- A camera button saves a postcard: a picture of the sky as it is, with the
  place and show number.
- Tap or click the sky to fire one shell of your own.
- A small caption names the act that is playing ("Act 2 of 6 · Willows").
- "Copy link" gives a link that opens the same place, colours and show.
- Ambient (TV) mode hides everything but the show and keeps the screen awake.
- A link preview picture for chat apps, and it can be installed as an app
  on a phone's home screen (see "Share it and install it").
- Works on phones and tablets. Portrait screens get their own composition
  with every landmark fully in view.
- Respects "reduce motion": gentler show, no flashing, no strobe.

Keys: `Space` launch a show, `M` mute, `P` postcard, `F` fullscreen, `A` ambient mode, `Esc` leave ambient mode.

### Volume

The slider sits above the mute button. With a mouse, point at the mute
button. With the keyboard, press `Tab` until the slider has focus, then use
the arrow keys. On a phone, tap the mute button: the slider opens for a few
seconds.

- Full (the default) is exactly as loud as BoomVoyage was before the slider.
  It cannot go louder.
- At 0 it is silent, the same as mute. The mute button then brings full
  volume back.
- It changes everything at once: fireworks, the night ambience and the
  quiet life sounds.
- The level is remembered on this device.

### Postcard

The camera button (or `P`) saves a PNG picture of the sky as it is at that
moment, on a paper-coloured card with the BoomVoyage name, the place and the
show number, for example "Paris · Show No. 4", and the country and date.
No personal names or addresses are put on it.

- On a phone or tablet the share sheet opens with the picture, if the
  browser can share files. Otherwise the picture is downloaded as
  `boomvoyage-paris-show-4.png`.
- The picture uses every pixel the screen has, so it is sharp on high-DPI
  screens. Its longest side is at most 1920 pixels.
- The buttons, the countdown and the captions are not in the picture.

## Link options

Add these to the address of the page (after a `?`, joined with `&`).

| Option | Example | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `scene` | `?scene=paris` | Opens that place: `sydney`, `dubai`, `paris` or `london`. Without it, the place you chose last time opens, or Sydney the first time. |
| `seed` | `?seed=3735928559` | The first show is this exact show: same acts, same timing. A whole number from 0 to 4294967295. |
| `theme` | `?theme=ember-jade` | Uses that colour theme for the shared show. It is the theme name in lower case with dashes, for example `harbour-blue`. |
| `ambient` | `?ambient=1` | Starts in ambient (TV) mode. Also works with `true`, `yes`, `on`. Use `0` for off. |
| `debug` | `?debug=1` | Shows a small frame-rate meter (see "Check the speed" below). |

Examples:

- `https://<your-user-name>.github.io/boomvoyage/?scene=dubai`
- `https://<your-user-name>.github.io/boomvoyage/?scene=london&ambient=1`
- `https://<your-user-name>.github.io/boomvoyage/?scene=paris&seed=5&theme=lavande`
- `https://<your-user-name>.github.io/boomvoyage/?scene=sydney&seed=1532556838&theme=harbour-blue`

- **Copy link** (the link button next to "Launch now") builds a link with
  `scene`, `seed` and `theme` of the show you just watched. The person who opens
  it sees the same show first. After that, shows are random as usual.
  The text "Link copied" appears for a moment. If the browser does not allow
  copying, a small box shows the link so you can copy it by hand.
- Options you add by hand can be mixed: `?scene=sydney&seed=42&ambient=1`.
- A wrong value (for example a seed that is not a number) is ignored. It never
  breaks the page. An unknown `scene` is ignored and no shared show is used.
- "Same show" holds for the same version of the site and the same
  reduce-motion setting (reduce motion swaps flashing bursts for calm ones).

### Ambient (TV) mode

Press the screen button next to "Copy link", press `A`, or open a link with
`?ambient=1`. All buttons, the countdown and the captions fade away, and the
mouse pointer is hidden. **Tap or click anywhere, or press `Esc`, to leave.**

- Browsers only allow sound after a tap, so a link with `?ambient=1` still
  shows "Tap to start" first. One tap, then the controls stay hidden.
- The screen is kept awake with the Screen Wake Lock API. Browsers without it
  (or that refuse it) get a best-effort fallback: a tiny silent video that
  some browsers treat as "something is playing". If neither works, nothing
  breaks, but the screen may dim; then set a longer screen timeout in the
  device settings. The lock is let go when you leave ambient mode.

### Hear the life sounds

With `?debug=1` in the address, press `1` (horn), `2` (water) or `3`
(chime) to hear a life sound at once, in the place you are in. A small
message names the sound, or says why it did not play (muted, or two are
already playing).

### Check the speed

Open the page with `?debug=1` and start a show. A small green box shows, for
the last ten seconds:

- **fps**: frames per second (higher is better; 60 or more is smooth).
- **typical**: the usual time for one frame in milliseconds (16.7 ms = 60 fps).
- **slow**: 95% of the frames are faster than this. This is what you feel as lag.
- **worst**: the slowest single frame.
- the number of sparks, the canvas size, the screen's pixel ratio, and the
  size and quality step of the fireworks layer.

If a browser feels slow, these numbers (best read during the finale) say how slow.

## Share it and install it

### Link preview

When you paste the link into WhatsApp, Messenger, Instagram, X and similar
apps, they show a picture, a title and a short text. The picture is
`img/preview.png` (1200x630): the four cities side by side with fireworks
and the BoomVoyage name.

- **One picture for every link.** A link with `?scene=paris` shows the same
  picture as the plain link. These apps read the page without running its
  code, and a static site sends the same page for every address, so it
  cannot change the preview per place.
- The addresses in the preview tags are full addresses on
  `https://aceasif.github.io/boomvoyage/`. If the site moves, change them in
  `index.html` (the tests name the address in `tests/site.test.js`).
- Apps remember a preview for days. To see a new one sooner, add something
  to the end of the link, for example `?v=2`.
- The picture was made by a small browser script that opens the running
  site, fires a few shells in each city and puts the four pictures
  together. The script is not part of the project; only the PNG is.

### Install as an app

BoomVoyage can sit on a phone's home screen and open full-screen, without
the browser's bars. There is no install button in the page; the browser
already has one.

- **Android (Chrome):** open the site, tap the menu (three dots), then
  "Add to Home screen" and "Install" (older versions say "Install app").
- **iPhone or iPad (Safari):** open the site, tap the Share button, then
  "Add to Home Screen".
- **Computer (Chrome or Edge):** click the install icon at the right end of
  the address bar.

It still needs the internet: it is the same website, opened from an icon.

## Run it

The page uses JavaScript modules, so it needs a small local web server.
Opening `index.html` by double-click will not work.

In PowerShell, inside the project folder:

```powershell
py -m http.server 8000
```

Then open http://localhost:8000 and press "Tap to start".

## Deploy to GitHub Pages

There is no build step, so GitHub can serve the repository as it is.

1. Merge the code into `main` on GitHub.
2. Open the repository on GitHub, then **Settings → Pages**.
3. Under **Build and deployment**, set **Source** to **Deploy from a branch**.
4. Choose branch **main** and folder **/ (root)**, then **Save**.
5. Wait about a minute. The site is at `https://<your-user-name>.github.io/boomvoyage/`.

All paths in the project are relative, so it works in that sub-folder.
The empty `.nojekyll` file tells GitHub Pages to serve the files unchanged.

## Add a place

A scene is one config object in one file. The four that exist are good models.

1. Copy the scene that is closest to your idea, for example
   `js/scenes/dubai.js` to `js/scenes/tokyo.js`.
2. Change `id`, `name`, `country`, `tagline`, the `palette`, and the drawing
   functions (`drawSky`, `drawSkyline`, `drawWater`). Write the skyline as two
   halves, `drawBackdrop` (far things) and `drawForeground` (the landmarks in
   front), and let `drawSkyline` call one after the other. The start screen, the
   "Stop 0N" label, the page title and the switcher all read these.
3. Set the framing: `horizon`, `focusX` and `portraitSpan` (how many scene
   units must fit across a phone held upright). Give portrait its own
   arrangement if the landmarks do not fit side by side; `shifted()` from
   `kit.js` moves and resizes a group.
4. Give the show its own flavour in `show`:
   - `palettes`: the colour themes and their names.
   - `acts`: which middle acts may be picked (three are picked per show).
     Choose from chase, mirror, fans, willows, garden, crossfire, sparkle,
     fountain, palms, waltz, chimes.
   - `opening`: classic, tower, duet or strikes.
   - `finaleTypes`: the burst shapes the finale mixes.
   - `actLabels`: the caption for each act, in any language.
   - The quiet middle act: either `features: { waterfall: true }` with a
     `waterfall` line from `rig()`, or `signature`: `spire`, `glitter` or `wheel`.
5. Return the anchor points of the signature act from `rig(v)`, in pixels:
   - `spire` needs `tiers` (points with a `level` and an `angle`) and `spire`.
   - `glitter` needs `tower` (points on the landmark) and `top`.
   - `wheel` needs `wheel` (points around a rim) and `hub`.
6. Tune `sound` if you like (reverb length, echo, boom pitch, ambience).
7. Give it some life between shows: a `life` list (see below).
8. Add the file to `js/scenes/index.js`:

   ```js
   import london from './london.js';
   import tokyo from './tokyo.js';
   export const scenes = [sydney, dubai, paris, london, tokyo];
   ```

The scene switcher, the start screen, link options, the schedule and the
sound pick it up with no other change. Run `node --test`: the tests in
`tests/scenes.test.js` check every scene in the list, the new one too.

### Life events

A scene's `life` list holds the small things that happen while the countdown
runs. `js/life.js` decides when; the scene only says what each one looks like.

```js
life: [
  {
    id: 'ferry',            // a name, unique in this scene
    layer: 'front',         // 'behind' or 'front' (see below); must be given
    group: 'water',         // two events of one group never show together
    weight: 2,              // how often it is picked (1 if left out)
    duration: [24, 32],     // shortest and longest time on screen, in seconds
    sound: 'horn',          // optional: 'horn', 'water' or 'chime'
    calm: false,            // optional: skip it when "reduce motion" is on
    setup: (r) => ({ depth: 0.1 + r() * 0.2 }),   // random choices, made once
    draw(ctx, v, e) { /* draw it; e.u runs from 0 to 1 */ },
  },
],
```

In `draw`, `e.u` is how far through its life the event is (0 to 1), `e.t` is
seconds since it began, `e.dir` is 1 or -1 (which way it travels), `e.p` is
what `setup` returned and `e.calm` is true with "reduce motion". Fading in
and out is done for you. Use `r()` from `setup` for anything random, never
`Math.random()`. `kit.js` has helpers: `vessel()` draws a boat, `crossing()`
moves something across the screen, `afloat()` puts it on the water, `glow()`
and `glint()` draw small lights. Give a scene at least three events in at
least two groups.

Every event must say its `layer`:

- `'behind'`: drawn over the sky and the scene's `drawBackdrop`, but before
  its `drawForeground`. The foreground landmarks cover it. Use it for things
  that pass behind a landmark: cars on a bridge behind a building, a plane or
  birds behind a tower, lights on far buildings.
- `'front'`: drawn over everything, the water too. Use it for boats and for
  lights that sit on a foreground landmark itself.

Look at the phone layout too: landmarks stand in different places there.

Scenes draw in "scene units": x runs from -500 to 500 and y goes up from the
waterline. `v.px(x)` and `v.py(y)` turn units into pixels, so one drawing
fits every screen size.

## Creative decisions

### Design

- **A night postcard, not a photo.** The scene is flat, layered silhouettes,
  like a cut-paper travel poster. This style stays sharp on any screen, costs
  almost nothing to draw, and lets the fireworks be the brightest thing.
- **Colour.** Deep indigo sky that turns to warm plum at the horizon, where
  the city lights are. The only pale object is the Opera House, so the eye
  finds it first. The only accent colour in the UI is one warm "ember" gold.
- **The scene reacts to the show.** Each burst lights the sky in its own
  colour for a moment, and the sails and windows catch that light. Fireworks
  and skyline are mirrored in the water, and the water moves. The light is
  only ever added, never blended in a way that can darken, so the sky stays
  clean.
- **Small true details.** The Southern Cross and its two pointer stars are in
  the sky, there is a thin moon, a red beacon on top of the arch, and small
  boats waiting on the water.
- **The Opera House.** Drawn in code as two halls of pointed shells. The
  shells lean toward the harbour and get taller toward it, and each hall has
  one small shell that faces the land. The far hall is larger and a little
  greyer, so the near one stands out. Thin rib lines fan over each shell,
  the open ends are warm glass walls, and a small pair of shells (the
  restaurant) sits at the land end of a wide three-step podium.
- **Two compositions.** On wide screens the city, the Opera House and the
  bridge stand in a row. On tall phone screens the scene is re-arranged like
  the classic postcard: the whole bridge fills the width, the Opera House
  sits in front of its left side, and the city is a small far-away row
  behind the sails. Nothing is cut off, and about four fifths of the sky is
  left for the fireworks, which are drawn bigger and burst higher there.
- **The waterfall on a phone.** On wide screens the waterfall pours from the
  bridge deck, as in the real show. In portrait the Opera House stands in
  front of the left half of the deck and would hide half of it. So there
  the curtain hangs from the arch: its whole top edge is in view above the
  sails, and the sparks fall behind the Opera House like a lit backdrop.
- **Typography.** A high-contrast serif in italic (Bodoni / Didot family) for
  the name and the numbers, like an old travel poster, and small wide-spaced
  capitals for labels. These are system fonts, so no font file is downloaded.
- **Volume without clutter.** The slider is not a seventh control in the
  row. It opens above the mute button when you point at it, focus it or (on
  a phone) tap it, and goes away again. The slider shows what you hear:
  while muted it rests at 0, and unmuting brings your level back. The curve
  is squared, so the middle of the slider sounds about half as loud.
- **The postcard is a print.** Warm paper, a hairline round the picture, the
  name set as on the site ("Voyage" in a darker gold that reads on paper),
  and the caption in italic. Wide pictures put the name left and the caption
  right; tall phone pictures stack the words in the middle. It is drawn only
  when the button is pressed, so the show never pays for it.
- **Quiet UI.** A few small round controls only. They sit in the corners and
  dim after six seconds without input, so the show has the whole screen.
  On a phone they form two short rows at the bottom, under the thumb.
- **Act captions.** A small gold "Act 2 of 6" over the act name in the same
  italic serif, just above the controls. It fades out and in when the act
  changes, and does not dim with the controls, so a quiet glance always tells
  you where the show is. With reduced motion the words change at once.
- **Ambient mode.** For a screen on a wall: no buttons, no numbers, no
  captions, no pointer. Only a three-second hint ("tap or press Esc to
  leave") appears when you switch it on by hand.

### The four places

Every place uses the same engine, but each was given its own mood, so that
changing the place feels like travelling and not like changing a wallpaper.

- **Sydney Harbour: warm and wide.** Indigo sky turning plum, the white
  sails of the Opera House, the long arch of the bridge. Classic acts with
  plain English names. Signature: the waterfall from the bridge.
- **Dubai: tall and precise.** A teal desert night with amber dust on the
  horizon, two searchlight beams and a new moon. The Burj Khalifa stands
  alone in the middle, silver-blue and far taller than everything else; its
  wings step back at different heights, which is what makes its outline.
  The golden Dubai Frame, blade and sail towers and palms keep it company.
  The show is choreographed like the fountain below the tower: rows of jets
  that cross ("Dancing Fountain"), a grove of palm bursts, and it opens with
  one huge gold shell. Signature, "The Spire": sparks climb the tower
  setback by setback, the facade glitters, then fans shoot from the tip.
  Themes: Desert Gold, Emerald & Pearl, Sapphire, Rose Gold, Arabian Night,
  Falcon. Sound: deeper booms and a late echo, like open desert air.
- **Paris: soft and romantic.** The blue hour: deep blue above, rose at the
  horizon, thin clouds, a full moon. The Eiffel Tower is a plain warm-gold
  lit silhouette with a hint of iron lattice; it does not copy the real
  tower's own light show. In front, a five-arch stone bridge with lamps and
  gilded columns; behind, Haussmann houses with attic windows, the gold dome
  of Les Invalides, and Sacré-Cœur on its hill. The show dances: "La Valse"
  is in three-four time, the opening is a duet of willows, and the act names
  are French. Signature, "Scintillement": glints of light on the iron that
  swell and fade in slow pulses, with gold willows beside the tower.
  Themes: Champagne, Bleu Blanc Rouge, La Vie en Rose, Lavande, Absinthe,
  Nuit Bleue. Sound: a short bright tail, as between stone houses.
- **London: moody and grand.** A low cloudy sky with the moon behind cloud
  and almost no stars. Cold against warm: the London Eye in blue-white
  light on the left, Elizabeth Tower and the Houses of Parliament in honey
  floodlight on the right, and green Westminster Bridge with a red bus
  between them. The clock shows midnight. The show is heavy and stately:
  it opens with three slow strikes, and "The Chimes" is four great shells
  like a bell. Signature, "The Wheel": small bursts run around the rim one
  way, then the other, then the whole rim fires at once. Themes: Union,
  Royal Purple, Thames Mist, Poppy, Emerald Park, Crown Jewels. Sound: full
  booms, a clear echo over the river, and a far bell now and then.
- **Life between shows.** The two minutes between shows should feel like a
  real evening, not a paused picture. So in the quiet time small things
  happen: one every 10 to 25 seconds, never more than two at once, never the
  same one twice in a row. They are small and slow on purpose; the fireworks
  stay the event. Each fades out at least 2.5 seconds before the next show
  (and within about a second if you press "Launch now"), and none starts
  while sparks are still in the air. Some come with one very quiet sound
  (a distant horn, a few laps of water, three soft notes) that the mute
  button silences with everything else.
  - **Sydney:** a green-and-cream harbour ferry with lit windows crosses the
    whole view; a small yacht drifts; a shooting star; office windows switch
    on and off while car lights cross the bridge; the red beacon on top of
    the arch glows brighter.
  - **Dubai:** a wooden dhow with a lantern-lit sail glides across the lake;
    an abra (water taxi) works along the shore; a plane's lights blink across
    the sky; a band of light climbs the Burj Khalifa from plaza to spire; the
    lake fountain practises with a row of thin white jets.
  - **Paris:** a long sightseeing boat with every window lit crosses under
    the bridge; a small boat with one lantern drifts; a few dozen soft lights
    come and go on the tower (our own simple effect, much quieter than the
    "Scintillement" act and not a copy of the real tower's light show); birds
    cross the evening sky; a thin rose cloud drifts.
  - **London:** a river bus crosses the Thames; a small tug works upstream;
    brighter lights travel slowly round the wheel; the clock face glows like
    a slow breath; a second red bus crosses Westminster Bridge.
- **Life sounds are planned, then played.** Each one is written down as
  plain numbers first (which tones, when, how the volume moves) and only then
  built with audio nodes. Every volume starts at zero, moves only in fades of
  30 ms or more and ends at zero, and every tone is stopped right after. At
  most two play at once, and they fade out in a third of a second when a show
  begins. This came from a real bug: the first water sound let raw noise
  through at full volume for a second or two before its fade began, and the
  first horn was so low and soft that nobody could hear it.
- **Life has its own dice.** Life events use their own random numbers, apart
  from the fireworks. A shared link still plays exactly the same show; only
  the small things around it differ, as they would on two real evenings.
- **Life and reduce motion.** With that setting on there is one event at a
  time, every 18 to 40 seconds. The fastest ones (shooting star, fountain)
  are left out, and the rest lose their blinking: the plane's strobe is off,
  the tower lights stay steady, the birds glide, the wheel lights stand still.
- **Life has depth.** Each event is either behind the landmarks or in front
  of everything. Each skyline is painted in two halves, a backdrop and a
  foreground, and "behind" events are drawn between them. So the Opera House
  hides the cars on the bridge deck behind it, on a phone it also hides the
  city windows behind its sails, the plane in Dubai and the birds in Paris
  pass behind the tower, and on a phone the lights at the foot of the London
  Eye go behind Westminster Bridge. The two halves are only painted when a
  "behind" event is on screen; the rest of the time the skyline is the same
  single picture as before.
  - **Sydney:** behind: shooting star, city lights and bridge cars, bridge
    beacon. Front: ferry, yacht.
  - **Dubai:** behind: plane. Front: dhow, abra, light on the Burj Khalifa,
    fountain.
  - **Paris:** behind: birds, cloud. Front: sightseeing boat, lantern boat,
    tower sparkle.
  - **London:** behind: wheel lights. Front: river bus, tug, clock glow,
    red bus.
- **Life pauses with the tab.** Its clock only moves when a frame is drawn,
  so a hidden tab pauses it. Ambient mode keeps it running.
- **One landmark is the star.** In each new place one landmark carries the
  signature effect, and the phone layout is built around it: on a phone the
  Burj and the Eiffel Tower stand in the middle and fill half the sky, and
  in London the wheel moves in beside the clock tower.
- **Glitter sits in front.** All other fireworks are drawn behind the
  skyline. Only the glitter is drawn in front of it, because it has to sit
  on the tower itself.

### Show

- **Five parts, like a real display:** an opening of slow single shells,
  three middle acts, the quiet waterfall, a finale, and one last gold shell.
- **Different every time.** Each show picks one of six named colour themes
  (Ember & Jade, Harbour Blue, Jacaranda, Southern Gold, Coral Sea, Waratah)
  and three of seven middle acts, in random order. It never uses the same
  theme twice in a row. The theme name appears on screen as the show's title.
- **The waterfall is the calm in the middle.** Sparks pour from the bridge
  deck, starting in the centre and spreading out, while slow white shells
  float above. Then comets fan out from the arch. After that the finale
  feels bigger.
- **The finale gets faster,** from about two shells a second to about nine,
  then five gold willows fill the sky together, three white flashes, a short
  pause, and a single very large gold shell to end.
- **Length.** A show runs about 47 seconds, so there is more than a minute
  of quiet night before the next one.

### Sound

- **All synthesised.** A boom is three layers: a low sine "thump" that falls
  in pitch, a noise rumble that gets darker as it fades, and a very short
  sharp crack. Bigger shells are lower and longer.
- **Space.** Every sound goes through a long reverb and one clear echo, as if
  it came back from the far shore. Sounds are placed left or right to match
  where the shell is on screen.
- **Synced to the picture.** The sound engine does not keep its own timeline.
  The drawing code tells it "this shell opened now", so the boom starts on
  the same frame as the burst.
- **Restraint.** Only some rockets whistle. At most three booms can start in
  the same tenth of a second, so the finale is strong but not distorted.
- **Night ambience.** Soft water that rises and falls, a little moving air,
  and a far-away buoy bell now and then. It is quiet on purpose: it makes the
  two minutes of waiting feel like part of the place.

### Technical choices

- **Timing uses timestamps.** The countdown is always "next start time minus
  now". If the tab is in the background and a show time passes, one show
  plays when you come back, and the next is a full two minutes later.
- **60 fps.** Particles are capped (1800 on desktop, 1000 on phones, 500 with
  reduced motion). Trail sparks are refused first when the cap is near, so
  the main stars always appear. The static scene is drawn once and reused.
- **Trails that really end.** Trails are made by dimming the fireworks layer
  a little every frame. Dimming alone never reaches black with 8-bit pixels:
  very faint values round back to themselves and stay as a ghost haze. So
  the layer is also reduced by one brightness step sixty times a second.
  Three seconds after a burst the sky is exactly as it was before.
- **Share links hold the show, not a recording.** A show is built from one
  number (its seed) plus its colour theme, so a link only needs those. The
  theme is in the link because a show avoids the previous show's colours,
  which the person opening the link has not seen.
- **Fast in every browser, measured.** Firefox ran the finale at about
  21 frames per second where Chrome ran 144 (same machine, 1600 px wide
  window, pixel ratio 1.5). Switching drawing parts off one at a time showed
  the cause: tinting the skyline with the burst colour every frame used
  blend modes that Firefox does not run on the graphics card. Now the
  skyline is split once into a red, a green and a blue picture, and each
  frame only adds the three with the strength of the burst colour: the same
  picture for a fraction of the cost. The second cost was the size of the
  fireworks layer on high-DPI screens.
- **Automatic quality, no browser names.** While fireworks are in the air
  the page watches its real frame times. Only if the browser is clearly
  behind does the fireworks layer get smaller (100%, then 80%, then 67%),
  copied across so nothing blinks. It never goes back up. Chrome keeps 100%.
  With both changes Firefox went from about 21 to about 92 frames per
  second in the same test, and Chrome stayed at 144.
- **All four places are equally fast.** Measured in the real browsers with a
  heavy load plus each place's signature act (window 1600x815, pixel ratio
  1.5, 144 Hz screen): Firefox about 100 frames per second in Dubai, Paris
  and London (typical frame 7.0 ms, slow frame 13.9 ms), and Chrome 144 in
  all of them. The skyline is drawn once and reused, whatever is on it.
- **Life between shows costs nothing you can measure.** Life events are a
  few small shapes drawn straight onto the picture each frame: no extra
  canvas, no particles, no blur. Measured in the real browsers with all five
  events of a place forced on at once (far more than the two that can ever
  show): Firefox 144 frames per second and Chrome 144, the same as with no
  life at all, in all four places. They never run during a show, so the
  fireworks are as fast as before: old and new code measured back to back
  gave the same numbers in Firefox and in Chrome.
- **No service worker.** Browsers no longer need one to offer "Install",
  so there is none. That means nothing is stored on the phone that could
  show an old version: every visit loads the site fresh, and a new deploy
  is seen at once. The price is that the app does not open without the
  internet, which is fine for a site this small.
- **Every path is relative.** The site lives in a sub-folder
  (`/boomvoyage/`), so the manifest uses `./` for its start page and scope
  and the icons are named without a leading slash. Only the link preview
  tags hold full addresses, because chat apps require them.
- **Full-screen when installed.** The manifest asks for `fullscreen`: on
  Android the app opens with no bars at all; iPhone gives its own
  full-screen mode, and the page already keeps its buttons clear of the
  clock and the home bar.
- **Icon.** A gold starburst on the night sky, like the small icon in the
  browser tab. The icons are solid squares; the phone cuts the corners
  itself, and the "maskable" one keeps the burst inside the safe middle.
- **Reduced motion.** With that system setting on, shows are slower and
  sparser, there is no strobe and no white flash, and the water is still.
